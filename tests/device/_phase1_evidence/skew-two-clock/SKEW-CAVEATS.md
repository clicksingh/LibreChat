# SKEW-CAVEATS — what the two-clock skew fixture does **not** claim

Companion to `SKEW-REPORT.md` (Part A). Every statement here is a limit of the evidence, stated so the find­ing is not over-read.

1. **Scope of the defect is the Mongo-side writers, not the transport.** The fixture exercises only the code paths that read `clock.now()` to decide *lease state* (`authorityService.js` `buildClaimFilter` / `releaseLapsedLeaseImpl` / `renewAuthorityImpl`, `pairingSessionService.js` `isLiveLease` / `isLapsedLease` / the challenge and consume CASes). It says nothing about the endpoint (±60 s) or relay (±120 s) clock policies, which remain pinned by R9/GM-16.
2. **The clock offsets are injected, not drift.** `Δ = ±300000 ms` is exact and deterministic. The fixture isolates the *predicate* from the clock source; it does not model NTP slew, step corrections, VM clock jitter, or real inter-host skew distributions. The measured horizon `Δ − 5000 ms` is therefore a bound implied by the predicate, not a measured field statistic.
3. **"Live" is defined against the fixture's reference timeline.** `TRUE_NOW` is the correct writer's injected clock, not the host's wall clock. No product decision or assertion reads `Date.now()`. A reader who wants "the lease was live on the machine's clock" must re-derive it from the seeded `expiresAt` and the two injected clock values, both of which are recorded per probe.
4. **The two probes are sequential, not simultaneous.** Both writers are barrier-armed before either acts, but `CORRECT` is released first and `OFFSET` second; the control's refusal is a no-op, so the treatment acts on the identical preimage. This fixture therefore does **not** test simultaneous two-writer contention — that property belongs to the existing E2-T7 / E2-T8 / E2-V24T15 fixtures.
5. **`SKEW-01-P3` proves a code can be minted, not accepted.** The full ceremony is driven through product bytes up to `approvePairingClaim` succeeding, so a live lease really is displaced by a new owner with a fresh 48 h lease. There is no endpoint in the loop, so the fixture does not claim the displaced endpoint's exact failure frame; the `LEASE_EPOCH_STALE` consequence is stated as the design's own behaviour (H01 §F-H01-3), not as an observation here.
6. **Only three write sites are probed.** `retireIdentity`, `spendChallenge` (beyond the ceremony), the sweep/repair path, and any HTTP-handler-level clock use are out of scope. A fourth skew-sensitive predicate elsewhere would not be caught by these fixtures.
7. **The renewal over-acceptance is bounded to the renewal path.** `SKEW-02` shows a slow writer accepting a renewal up to `Δ − 5000 ms` past true expiry. It does not quantify what an attacker could do with that window, nor whether the endpoint would present a matching proof; the proof verifier is an injected seam in this fixture.
8. **One database per fixture, no shared-state stress.** Each fixture owns a `cbhr_rdc_phase1_w3_*` database that is dropped afterwards; the server namespace audit is `before == after`. Cross-tenant or cross-database interference is not exercised.
9. **Reproduction count.** The three fixtures were executed repeatedly during development and the final run is the certified one; `SKEW-03` was additionally re-run end-to-end and produced the identical grid and horizon. The finding is deterministic under the fixture's own offsets, but that is not a claim about frequency in production, where the offset is whatever the deployed writer's clock happens to be.
10. **No product change.** Nothing was fixed, and the report intentionally does not prescribe a specific corrective design beyond naming the boundary (pin a clock authority / max acceptable skew, reject stale-clock writers, or derive expiry decisions from server-asserted time).
